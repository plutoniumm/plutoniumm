Correcting Quantum Errors 2: Staying on the Manifold

arXiv:2512.18061 • 19 December 2025

Part 1 built a gradient for quantum codes: fidelity is a black box evaluated by running the encode → noise → recover → decode pipeline, each complex coefficient ai=xi+iyia_i = x_i + i y_iai​=xi​+iyi​ is two real knobs, each knob gives a slope by finite differences. The update that falls out steps every coefficient at once:

ai  →  ai−η(∂L∂xi+i ∂L∂yi) a_i \;\rightarrow\; a_i - \eta\left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial x_i} + i\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial y_i} \right) ai​→ai​−η(∂xi​∂L​+i∂yi​∂L​)

That update treats the 128 real numbers of a [[5,1,3]][[5,1,3]][[5,1,3]] code as free. They are not. A pair of codewords is only a code if each has unit length and the two are perpendicular,

∥ ∣0L⟩ ∥=1,∥ ∣1L⟩ ∥=1,⟨0L∣1L⟩=0 \| \, |0_L\rangle \, \| = 1, \qquad \| \, |1_L\rangle \, \| = 1, \qquad \langle 0_L | 1_L \rangle = 0 ∥∣0L​⟩∥=1,∥∣1L​⟩∥=1,⟨0L​∣1L​⟩=0

This section is about how we maintain the physics of the codewords and the space by using Riemannian geometry to stay on the constraint surface.

Four equations, 124 directions

Count what the constraints remove. Two codewords of 32 complex coefficients are 128 real numbers. The two norm conditions are one real equation each. The overlap ⟨0L∣1L⟩\langle 0_L | 1_L \rangle⟨0L​∣1L​⟩ is a complex number, so forcing it to zero is two real equations. Four equations in 128 unknowns leave a surface with 128−4=124128 - 4 = 124128−4=124 dimensions, curved and sitting inside the flat 128-dimensional space the optimiser actually moves in.

A smooth constraint surface like this is called a manifold: zoom in far enough at any point and it looks flat, and that local flat approximation is called the tangent space at the point. The simplest example is already here: a single normalised codeword is 64 real numbers with ∑i(xi2+yi2)=1\sum_i (x_i^2 + y_i^2) = 1∑i​(xi2​+yi2​)=1, a sphere in 63 dimensions. The full feasible set, pairs of orthonormal vectors, is a Stiefel manifold.

Traditional Gradient descent generates points wherever the arithmetic lands, whereas optimising on the surface closes a critical gap: every iterate is a valid code, so every score is a real fidelity.

A circle to think with

Every failure and every fix already happens in two dimensions, where it can be drawn. Take a point q=(x,y)q = (x, y)q=(x,y) constrained to the unit circle x2+y2=1x^2 + y^2 = 1x2+y2=1 (the manifold) and score it with f(x,y)=x+2yf(x, y) = x + 2yf(x,y)=x+2y. Two geometric tools fix this, and both are one line of algebra on the circle. First, the tangent space: at a point qqq on the circle, the directions that stay on the circle to first order are the ones perpendicular to qqq. Project the gradient onto them by subtracting its radial part:

gtan=∇f−(∇f⋅q) q=(1,2)−1⋅(1,0)=(0, 2)at q=(1,0) g_{\text{tan}} = \nabla f - (\nabla f \cdot q)\, q = (1,2) - 1 \cdot (1, 0) = (0,\ 2) \quad \text{at } q = (1,0) gtan​=∇f−(∇f⋅q)q=(1,2)−1⋅(1,0)=(0, 2)at q=(1,0)

Stepping along (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2) instead of (1,2)(1, 2)(1,2) gives q′=(1,0.4)q' = (1, 0.4)q′=(1,0.4), with r=1.16=1.077r = \sqrt{1.16} = 1.077r=1.16​=1.077. Better, but still off the circle: the tangent line is straight and the circle curves away from it, so a straight step of size η\etaη leaks off the manifold by order η2\eta^2η2. No direction fixes curvature, hence the second tool. A retraction pulls the landed point back onto the manifold.

While in the general case a retraction implies taking a QRQRQR decomposition, for a sphere (or an n-ball) it reduces simply to division by the norm:
q′′=(1, 0.4)1.077=(0.928, 0.371),f(q′′)=1.671 q'' = \frac{(1,\ 0.4)}{1.077} = (0.928,\ 0.371), \qquad f(q'') = 1.671 q′′=1.077(1, 0.4)​=(0.928, 0.371),f(q′′)=1.671

That 1.671 used both tools: project, then step, then retract. Is the projection even needed, or does the retraction alone do the job? Test it by retracting the raw step from before, the one that landed at (1.2,0.4)(1.2, 0.4)(1.2,0.4) with r=1.265r = 1.265r=1.265:

(1.2, 0.4)1.265=(0.949, 0.316),f=0.949+2⋅0.316=1.581 \frac{(1.2,\ 0.4)}{1.265} = (0.949,\ 0.316), \qquad f = 0.949 + 2 \cdot 0.316 = 1.581 1.265(1.2, 0.4)​=(0.949, 0.316),f=0.949+2⋅0.316=1.581

Same start, same η\etaη, worse score: 1.581 against 1.671. The raw step spent part of its length pushing radially outward, and the retraction deleted that part on landing. Projecting first puts the whole step into a direction that survives, so nothing is wasted. The same three moves, in 128 dimensions

Nothing in project, step, retract cared that the circle was two-dimensional. For codes the point is the codeword pair, the sphere becomes the Stiefel manifold, and the three moves run verbatim. The gradient from part 1 arrives as one slope vector gwg_wgw​ per codeword. Project: the components of gwg_wgw​ along the codewords themselves spend their length changing norms and overlap rather than fidelity, so subtract both:

g~w=gw  −  ∣0L⟩⟨0L∣gw⟩  −  ∣1L⟩⟨1L∣gw⟩,w∈{0L,1L} \tilde g_w = g_w \;-\; |0_L\rangle\langle 0_L | g_w \rangle \;-\; |1_L\rangle\langle 1_L | g_w \rangle, \qquad w \in \{0_L, 1_L\} g~​w​=gw​−∣0L​⟩⟨0L​∣gw​⟩−∣1L​⟩⟨1L​∣gw​⟩,w∈{0L​,1L​}

Step: ai→ai+η g~ia_i \rightarrow a_i + \eta\, \tilde g_iai​→ai​+ηg~​i​ for every coefficient at once.

Retract: divide ∣0L⟩|0_L\rangle∣0L​⟩ by its norm, subtract from ∣1L⟩|1_L\rangle∣1L​⟩ its component along the new ∣0L⟩|0_L\rangle∣0L​⟩, divide that by its norm. Two norm divisions and one subtraction, one per line below, worked on a pair that has drifted off the manifold:

The result is orthonormal. Every iterate is a valid code, every reported score is then a real valid fidelity, by its very structure.

Any evaluator will do

Nothing in the loop ever asked what fidelity is. The projection and the retraction are plain linear algebra, and F\mathcal{F}F only ever appears as "evaluate, twice per knob". So the evaluator is swappable: Monte Carlo sampling for codes too large to simulate exactly, tensor-network estimates, or direct fidelity estimation on hardware, where descent tunes codewords against the device's true noise, including what no model contains. The cost is dimension: pure states on nnn qudits of dimension ddd carry O(dn)\mathcal{O}(d^n)O(dn) coefficients, so the knob count grows exponentially and million-qubit codes are out of reach. The realistic targets are small codes tailored to measured, lopsided noise, the regime where hand-designed codes leave the most fidelity on the table.
