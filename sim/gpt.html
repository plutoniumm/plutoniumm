Generalised Probability

A generalised probabilistic theory keeps the rules of probability and drops everything else. A single system is a convex body: points are states, corners are pure states, mixtures are line segments. A two-outcome measurement is an affine function e with 0 ≤ e ≤ 1 on the body, and e(ω) is the probability of one outcome. The shape of the body alone decides what the theory can do: a triangle is classical, a disc behaves like a qubit, a square allows stronger than quantum correlations. Framework: Barrett, quant-ph/0508211; polygon models: Janotta, Gogolin, Barrett, Brunner, 1012.1215.

Distinguishing game

A state is drawn fairly from {ω₁, ω₂}. You measure {e, u−e} above and guess ω₁ on outcome e. Success = ½[e(ω₁) + 1 − e(ω₂)]. Rotate the measurement above to change e.

CHSH vs shape

Maximal CHSH value S of the maximally entangled state of two such systems, from Janotta et al, eqs 22 and 23, maximised over the body's extremal effects. Even polygons reach or exceed Tsirelson's bound, odd polygons stay below it and climb towards it.

current shape: 5-gon, odd · S = 2.683

Presets

the simplex. two vertices, the effect that tells them apart with certainty, chsh stays at 2.
the disc. antipodal pure states are the only perfectly distinguishable pair, chsh reaches 2√2.
the square. two adjacent corners are perfectly distinguishable without being antipodal, chsh reaches 4.

Notes

  • drag ω₁ and ω₂ anywhere in the body. a dot on a corner (or on the disc's rim) is pure, anywhere else mixed. the slider mixes them; the black dot is the mixture.
  • drag the orange handle to rotate the measurement. the body's tint is the probability of the orange outcome: 1 at the orange end, 0 at the green end, ½ on the dashed line. the effect is kept tight, so it spans the full [0, 1] range on the body and is always valid.
  • three regimes from one knob. n = 3 is the classical trit: a simplex, unique decompositions, S = 2. the disc is the rebit, the real-amplitude qubit: only antipodal pure states are perfectly distinguishable, S = 2√2. the square is boxworld's gbit: adjacent corners are perfectly distinguishable without being antipodal, S = 4 (pr box).
  • the chsh curve uses the published polygon formulas only; no two-system state is simulated here. checked values: hexagon S = 3, octagon exactly 2√2, 12-gon 2√2 / cos(π/12) ≈ 2.928, pentagon ≈ 2.683.
